Create an FAQ from a Notice with AI: Leave Unsupported Answers as Needs Confirmation

This exercise turns a short notice into six FAQ entries. Questions supported by the source receive source IDs, while topics such as fees, refunds, and waitlists that do not appear in the notice are left as needs confirmation instead of being answered from assumptions.

Who this is forBeginners who want to organize common questions from an event, class, or meeting notice but are concerned that AI may invent answers beyond the source

What you need
  • You only need to be able to separate a notice into individual statements.
  • Add source IDs such as S1 and S2 before each statement.
  • No real personal data or actual organization information is required.

01Turn the notice into a fact list with source IDs

Before creating an FAQ, separate the facts that can actually be confirmed from the notice. The event in this exercise is entirely fictional, and only S1–S4 should be treated as official input. In particular, there are no statements about participation fees, refunds, or a waitlist. Explicitly marking these gaps helps prevent AI from filling them with common event practices.

Sample input
S1 Event date and time: 11-20 at 16:00
S2 Participation: advance registration required, capacity 16 people
S3 What to bring: writing utensils
S4 Online participation: not provided

Items not included in the source:
- No fee information
- No refund information
- No waitlist information
source IDSource factWhat the FAQ can answer
S111-20 at 16:00Event date and start time
S2Advance registration required, capacity 16 peopleWhether registration is required and capacity
S3Writing utensilsWhat to bring
S4Online participation not providedWhether online participation is available

A source ID is not decoration. It is a verification marker. Someone reading the FAQ should be able to see S2 and immediately locate the original S2 statement.

02Design questions that include both answerable and unresolved topics

An FAQ does not need a definite answer for every question. You can include questions that readers are likely to ask and mark them as needs confirmation when the source does not contain the answer. In this exercise, you will create exactly six questions, with fees and refunds combined into one question.

Prompt
Create 6 FAQ entries from the notice below.

Rules:
- Use only information that actually appears in S1–S4.
- If an answer has supporting evidence, show the source ID at the end.
- If the source does not contain the answer, do not guess. Mark it as needs confirmation.
- Preserve the fact that fee, refund, and waitlist information is not provided in the source.
- Do not change online participation not provided into online participation being available.
- Do not change the date, time, or capacity number.

Use these 6 FAQ questions:
1. When is the event held?
2. Is advance registration required, and what is the capacity?
3. What should I bring?
4. Can I participate online?
5. What are the participation fee and refund policy?
6. If capacity is full, can I join a waitlist?

Because the questions themselves are fixed in this prompt, AI is less likely to add extra questions or omit important facts. Marking an answer as unknown is not a failure. It is a valid result when the source does not support an answer.

04Filter out plausible but unsupported FAQ answers

The main risk in automatic FAQ creation is turning common real-world practices into source-backed facts. Free admission, no-refund policies, and waitlist registration may be common in some situations, but none of them appear in this fictional notice. Plausible does not mean supported.

Flawed result
Q. What are the participation fee and refund policy?
A. Participation is free, so there is no separate refund process.

Q. If capacity is full, can I join a waitlist?
A. Yes. Once the 16-person capacity is reached, applicants are automatically placed on the waitlist.
Invented detailSource statusCorrect treatment
Participation is freeNo fee informationNeeds confirmation
No separate refund processNo refund informationNeeds confirmation
Automatic waitlist registrationNo waitlist informationNeeds confirmation

When revising, removing unsupported claims is more important than making the answer sound polished. Even if you want to add contact information after needs confirmation, do not invent a phone number or email address if the source does not contain one.

05Re-request correction only for answers without source support

When an unsupported FAQ answer appears, specify exactly which statement goes beyond the source. Do not ask AI to fill the gap with new information. Instead, ask it to clearly mark the information as absent from the source. Any answer without a source ID should be treated as something to review again.

Prompt
Revise only Q5 and Q6 from the previous FAQ.

The claims in Q5 that participation is free and that there is no refund process do not appear in the source. Change both fee and refund information to needs confirmation.
The automatic waitlist registration in Q6 also does not appear in the source. Change waitlist availability to needs confirmation.
Do not invent contact details, policies, or exceptions that are not in S1–S4.
Do not change the date, time, 16-person capacity, writing utensils, or online participation not provided in Q1–Q4.

This kind of re-request locks the response back to the source. Even after correction, check that Q1–Q4 were not unintentionally changed.

06Check the FAQ by source ID before publishing

An FAQ should be easy to read, but it should not contain more facts than the original notice. You can rephrase questions naturally, but changing dates, capacity, negative conditions, or unresolved items turns it into a different notice. Check each item below.

Checklist
[ ] Are there exactly 6 FAQ entries?
[ ] Does 11-20 at 16:00 match S1?
[ ] Do advance registration required and capacity 16 people match S2?
[ ] Is the required item still writing utensils?
[ ] Is the negative wording online participation not provided preserved?
[ ] Does the FAQ avoid saying free or paid when no fee information exists?
[ ] Does the FAQ avoid inventing a refund policy when none is provided?
[ ] Does the FAQ avoid guessing whether a waitlist is available?
[ ] Are unsupported answers marked needs confirmation?

When applying this method to a real notice, unsupported questions can also be collected in a separate list for a person in charge to verify before updating the source notice itself. This tutorial does not determine real organization policies or legal obligations. It only practices preserving facts within the fictional notice provided.

What to check yourself

Editorial example in which six FAQ entries were checked by a human against the fictional S1–S4 event notice and the explicitly missing fee, refund, and waitlist information

  • Check that there are exactly 6 FAQ entries
  • Check that the date, time, capacity, what-to-bring item, and online participation condition from S1–S4 are preserved
  • Check that no fee, refund, or waitlist information was invented
  • Check that unsupported questions are marked needs confirmation
Verification limits

The result is an educational editorial example created and reviewed with AI assistance. It is not a result of executing an actual AI product, measuring response accuracy, or verifying any specific service's FAQ-generation feature.
