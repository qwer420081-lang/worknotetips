AI Meeting Notes: Merge Multiple Attendees' Notes into Decisions, Action Items, and TBD Items

When creating AI meeting notes, instead of polishing only one person's notes, practice combining records from multiple attendees and organizing decisions, action items, TBD items, and differences between records together with source tags. Using fictional Team Haneul meeting notes from 2026-10-20, this guide shows how to create a draft for team sharing without forcing different wording into a single version.

What you need
  • A general AI chat tool that accepts text input
  • Prepare each attendee's notes for the same meeting separately
  • Use fictional or de-identified data instead of real confidential information, customer information, or contact details
  • Have someone compare the AI-organized version against the original notes

01Preserve sources when merging multiple notes

After a meeting, each attendee's notes emphasize different things. One person may record only decisions, while another leaves their own action items or wording that has not yet been confirmed. In AI meeting notes, the important point is not to make the sentences flow smoothly, but to preserve traceability so the team can see which note each item came from.

This article uses a fictional Team Haneul meeting held on 2026-10-20. The purpose of the meeting notes is not to invent decisions that are absent from the input, but to separate commonly confirmed facts, assigned tasks, TBD items, and differences between records so the team can review them again.

02Enter each attendee's notes with item numbers

Attach the attendee's name and an item number to the input material. Even when two notes appear to describe the same thing, keep different wording as written. This example includes one note that records the promotional-material deadline as an exact date and another that uses a relative expression.

The shared registration status is listed separately to keep the series background consistent, but the 2026-10-20 meeting notes themselves do not pull in a later registration count as though it were a decision made at the meeting. Distinguishing the time scope of inputs is also an important part of reviewing meeting notes.

03Ask to separate decisions, action items, TBD items, and record differences

In the prompt, specify classification criteria rather than asking only for a summary. In particular, when records differ, do not have AI choose the more plausible sentence and treat it as confirmed; require both sources to remain. Also ask that owners and deadlines be left as 'TBD' or 'Needs confirmation' when the input does not support them.

Prompt
Organize the attendee-by-attendee meeting notes above into one draft of AI meeting notes for team sharing.

Categories:
- Decisions or reconfirmed facts
- Action items: show an owner and deadline only when they appear in the input
- TBD or Needs confirmation
- Different records or wording differences
- Draft team-sharing message

Rules:
- Keep the original source tag that supports each item at the end of the item.
- Do not arbitrarily delete one of two different records or treat one as confirmed.
- Use the shared schedule and attendee notes as distinct sources.
- Do not invent owners, deadlines, or completion status that are not in the input.
- Do not present information from after the meeting as though it were a decision made during the meeting.
- Do not add real contact details or personal information.

A good merged version is not a document that chooses the most plausible note. It is a document that lines up the points the team needs to verify again. In particular, relative expressions such as 'sometime next week' should not be converted arbitrarily into a date; preserve the original wording. Even if the shared schedule contains an exact date, the wording difference between meeting notes can still be preserved as a record.

05Fix meeting notes that erase TBD items and record differences

Flawed result
[Common flawed result · not an actual AI output]
All venue discussions have been resolved, and the promotional-material schedule was agreed without disagreement. Each owner can proceed according to the existing plan, and there is nothing else to confirm.

This example removes the TBD status and record differences that remain in the input. The final venue has not yet been decided, and booking availability for Option B has not been confirmed. In addition, Junho's 'sometime next week' record and Seoyeon's exact-date record use different wording. Removing this difference makes it difficult to trace later who understood the schedule based on which evidence. To fix it, restore the TBD / Needs confirmation items and both source tags.

06Before sharing, have people verify the source text, timing, and owners

Before team sharing, having only one attendee read the organized version may not be enough. Check that no source items were omitted, that owners and deadlines actually appear in the notes, and that information created after the meeting has not been mixed in as though it were a decision made during the meeting. Important decisions are confirmed through the team's approval process, not by the wording of the meeting notes alone.

Checklist
Pre-share review checklist
- Are decisions, action items, TBD items, and record differences clearly separated?
- Does each key item retain its original note tag?
- Are owners and deadlines actually present in the input?
- Were different records kept separate rather than arbitrarily merged into one?
- Was post-meeting status kept from being presented as a decision made during the meeting?
- Are real confidential information, customer information, and contact details excluded?

The next step after merging notes from multiple people is to collect multiple people's progress updates under the same criteria. The next article, “AI Weekly Report: Roll Up Multiple Team Members' Progress into One Report” shows how to combine completed work, work in progress, delays, dependencies, help requests, and items needing confirmation in one report.

Verification limits

This is an edited example based on fictional notes, not meeting notes generated by an actual AI tool. In real meetings, people must compare each attendee's original notes with the latest status and separately follow the team's decision and approval process.
