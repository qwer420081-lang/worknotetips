Use AI to Prepare Presentation Questions and Short Answers

This exercise shows how to prepare 6 expected audience questions with source evidence, short answers, and a verification plan when the answer is unknown. It does not cover creating a presentation outline or AI voice evaluation; the focus is on answering questions without overstating limited evidence.

01Lock the Source Evidence Used for Presentation Practice

In this exercise, you prepare Q&A responses using only the numbers and unresolved items already provided, rather than inventing new presentation content. The invited group, attendee group, anonymous survey respondents, and respondents who want to participate again are different populations, so the denominator for each percentage must be kept clear.

Sample input
[Fictional presentation evidence]
S1: 24 people were invited, and 18 attended.
S2: Among the attendees, 12 responded to an anonymous survey, and 9 of those respondents said they wanted to participate again.
S3: The next program date and budget are still undecided.

Calculable value:
- Share of survey respondents who want to participate again: 9/12 = 75%

Notes:
- 75% is based on the 12 survey respondents.
- Do not say that 75% of all 18 attendees wanted to participate again.
- Do not apply the same percentage to all 24 invitees.
- Do not claim that an actual AI voice evaluation of the presentation was performed.

02Connect 6 Expected Audience Questions to the Source Evidence

Useful expected questions help the presenter practice the limits of what can be answered instead of forcing a guess about unknown information. For each question, identify whether S1, S2, or S3 is relevant, and if the available evidence is insufficient, preserve that uncertainty in the answer.

Prompt
Using only the fictional presentation evidence below, create 6 expected audience questions that could come up after the presentation.

S1: 24 people were invited, and 18 attended.
S2: Among the attendees, 12 responded to an anonymous survey, and 9 of those respondents said they wanted to participate again.
S3: The next date and budget are undecided.

For each question, provide these 4 items:
1. Expected question
2. Source ID from the original evidence
3. Short answer the presenter can give
4. Verification plan if the answer is currently unknown

Notes:
- State clearly that 9/12 = 75% is based on survey respondents.
- Do not extend 75% to all 18 attendees or all 24 invitees.
- Do not invent reasons, satisfaction levels, costs, or dates that are not in the input.
- Do not create a new presentation outline.
- Do not claim that an actual AI voice evaluation was performed.

04Divide an 8-Minute Practice Session into Explanation, Questions, and Closing

After preparing the questions and answers, practice saying them aloud. Here, the rehearsal plan uses 4 minutes for explanation, 3 minutes for questions, and 1 minute for closing, for a total of 8 minutes. This is a practice allocation, not a claim that an actual presentation will take exactly this amount of time. A person should measure the real duration and adjust it.

If AI writes something labeled as '4 minutes,' that does not prove the actual speaking time will be 4 minutes. Speaking speed, pauses, and question length vary, so a person must measure this directly.

05Find Exaggerated Numerical Claims and Invented Undecided Information

A key presentation-practice error is expanding a limited survey result to the entire attendee group or filling in undecided information as if it were known. The example below uses some correct numbers but mishandles the denominator and the unresolved status.

Flawed result
[Incorrect editorial example]
75% of attendees said they want to participate in the next program. Because strong interest in returning has been confirmed, the next program is expected to take place on the same weekday next month, and the budget will also be finalized at the previous level.

The 9 people are not 75% of all 18 attendees; they are 75% of the 12 survey respondents. The preferences of the 6 attendees who did not respond are unknown. In addition, S3 says the next date and budget are undecided, so there is no basis for adding 'the same weekday next month' or 'the previous budget level.'

Prompt
Rewrite the answer so it matches the original evidence.
- State that the denominator for 75% is the 12 survey respondents.
- Do not generalize the same percentage to all 18 attendees.
- Do not generalize it to all 24 invitees either.
- Keep the next date and budget marked as undecided.
- Do not invent reasons for absence, satisfaction, costs, or schedules that are not in the input.
- For unknown questions, answer 'the current evidence does not tell us' and state what should be checked.
- Do not claim that AI analyzed or evaluated an actual presentation recording.

06Checklist for the Presenter Before the Actual Presentation

Finally, read through the expected-question table and check the denominator and source ID for every number. During actual rehearsal, measure the time yourself, shorten answers that run long, and practice not guessing when you do not know an answer. The results in this article are editorial examples created and reviewed with AI assistance; they do not validate actual presentation outcomes, audience reactions, AI voice-evaluation accuracy, or any specific product feature.

Checklist
[Reader checklist]
□ Did I distinguish 24 invitees from 18 attendees?
□ Did I preserve that 12 of the attendees responded to the survey?
□ Did I confirm that 9 people who want to participate again are 9 of the 12 respondents?
□ When saying 9/12 = 75%, do I state that the percentage is based on respondents?
□ Did I avoid applying 75% to all 18 attendees?
□ Did I avoid applying 75% to all 24 invitees?
□ Did I keep the next date undecided?
□ Did I keep the budget undecided?
□ Are there exactly 6 expected questions?
□ Does each question include source evidence, a short answer, and a verification plan?
□ Did I confirm that 4 minutes explanation + 3 minutes questions + 1 minute closing = 8 minutes?
□ Is the actual rehearsal time to be measured by a person?
□ Did I avoid claiming that an actual AI voice evaluation was performed?

What to check yourself

Editorial-example review scope: checked that the numbers and unresolved status in S1-S3, the denominator of 9/12 = 75%, the 6 expected questions, and the 4+3+1 = 8-minute practice plan are internally consistent.

  • Distinguished 24 invitees from 18 attendees
  • Distinguished 12 survey respondents from 9 respondents who want to participate again
  • Expressed 75% only as a percentage of survey respondents
  • Included 6 expected audience questions
  • Kept the date and budget undecided
  • Confirmed 4 minutes explanation, 3 minutes questions, and 1 minute closing for a total of 8 minutes
  • Instructed the reader to measure actual duration personally
  • Made no claim that AI voice evaluation was performed
Verification limits

No actual presentation, audience Q&A, voice analysis, or specific AI product evaluation feature was run or validated. The questions and answers shown are editorial examples based on fictional evidence and do not predict actual presentation performance or audience response.
